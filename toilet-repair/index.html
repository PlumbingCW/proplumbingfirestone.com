A toilet is a simple machine. Water enters through a fill valve, drops through a flapper into the bowl, and leaves through a curved trap into the drain. Nearly every repair comes down to one of those parts, the seal under the bowl, or a blockage past the trap.

Most Firestone homes were built after 2000, so many toilets in ZIP codes 80504 and 80520 are likely low-flow models. Those handle heavy paper and wipes poorly, which is worth remembering before you plunge a third time.

Six toilet problems and what each one usually means

Most calls fit one of these patterns. A plumber confirms which one on site.

Water runs on and on.

The flapper is not sealing or the fill valve is stuck open.

Weak or half flush.

Clogged rim holes, a low tank level or a partial blockage.

Bowl rises, then drains slowly.

A partial clog in the trap or the branch line behind it.

Puddle around the base.

A failed wax ring, loose bolts, or a tank sweating in humid air.

Tank refills by itself.

A slow leak past the flapper that drops the water level.

Cracked porcelain.

Hairline cracks in a bowl or tank are not repairable and usually mean replacement.

Overflow or leak right now: what to do while you wait

Close the supply valve.

It is the small oval handle on the wall or floor behind the toilet. Turn it clockwise until it stops.

Hold the flapper down.

Lift the tank lid and press the rubber flapper into its seat. This stops more water entering the bowl.

Keep electricity away from the water.

Cords, heaters and floor outlets should stay clear. Wet bathroom floors and power do not mix.

Notice other fixtures.

If the tub gurgles or the sink drains slowly too, the problem is deeper in the line. Tell dispatch, since that may be a sewer line cleaning call.

Skip the drain chemicals.

Harsh cleaners can splash back and may damage the bowl or complicate the repair.

Repair the toilet or replace it

Plumbers weigh age, fit and how many parts have already failed.

Repair usually makes sense when

  • A fill valve or flapper has worn out.
  • The wax ring has failed but the bowl is sound.
  • A single clog caused the trouble.
  • The handle or chain is loose or broken.

Replacement is worth discussing when

  • The bowl or tank is cracked.
  • The flush stays weak after cleaning and adjusting.
  • Several internal parts are failing close together.
  • The floor flange is damaged beyond a simple fix.

A leak at the base and the wax ring

A wax ring is the soft seal between the toilet and the drain flange in the floor. When it fails, a little water seeps out at the base each time you flush. That is slow enough to soften the subfloor before anyone notices, so a rocking toilet or a stain on the ceiling below deserves attention.

The fix means lifting the toilet, cleaning the flange, setting a new ring and seating the bowl again. We look at the flooring around the flange while the toilet is off. If water has reached the room below, a leak detection visit may be the next step.

What Firestone's soft water means for toilet parts

In its latest report the Central Weld County Water District lists average hardness at 33 mg/L, a figure it describes as soft. Soft water leaves less mineral crust on fill valves, so scale is a less likely cause of a stuck valve. Its disinfectant is chlorine, which over time can stiffen rubber flappers, so check the flapper first when a toilet runs.

The Town buys water from more than one supplier, so your own address may differ. Ask which supplier serves you before assuming.

A new toilet or a bidet seat

Some calls start as repairs and end with a replacement. Setting a new toilet involves a fresh wax ring, a clean flange, new supply connections and a level, solid seat on the floor. A bidet seat needs a tee on the supply line and an outlet within reach, so tell us what is near the toilet when you call.

Larger fixture work belongs under plumbing installation, where jobs can be planned for a convenient day. New toilet installs follow the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC) as adopted in Colorado, which the Firestone building division enforces, so ask them whether your swap needs a permit.
