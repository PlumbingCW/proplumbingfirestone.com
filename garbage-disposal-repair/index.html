A garbage disposal that hums without spinning, stops dead, or leaks under the sink is a kitchen problem that grows fast. Our 24/7 emergency line handles garbage disposal repair in Firestone, CO, whether the unit is jammed, clogged or dripping from its body. While you wait, switch it off at the wall, keep your hand out of the drain, and stop running the sink if water is pooling in the cabinet.

A disposal is a small motor with a spinning plate and two lugs. It grinds soft scraps into pieces small enough to wash down the drain. It does not chew bone, fibrous peels, grease or coffee grounds in bulk, and most breakdowns trace back to one of those.

Homes in ZIP codes 80504 and 80520 vary in age, so the unit under your sink may be a builder-grade model or a later replacement. Check the label on the housing and tell us what it says, because mounting style and size change which parts fit.

What the symptom is telling you

The sound or leak usually narrows the cause before anyone opens the cabinet.

  • Loud hum, no spin.Something is jammed against the grinding plate, or the motor is struggling against it.
  • Silence when switched on.The unit may have tripped its reset, lost power at the switch, or burned out.
  • Water at the top ring.The flange seal or mounting has loosened.
  • Water from the side or bottom.A cracked housing or a failed internal seal. This usually means replacement.
  • Slow sink with the disposal on.A clog beyond the unit, in the trap or the branch line.

Jams, resets and what is safe to try yourself

Most units have a hex socket in the bottom and a small red reset button on the underside. Power must be off before you touch either one. With the switch off, a wooden spoon handle pushed against a blade can free a stuck lug, and the reset button restores a unit that overheated.

Never put a hand in the drain, even with the power off, and do not pour drain chemicals into the unit. If the reset trips again right away, or the motor stays dead, the motor has failed or something is deeper than you can reach. Call us then.

Dishwasher line, switch and wiring

Most dishwashers drain through the disposal. If water stands in the dishwasher after a cycle, the drain hose connection or a knockout plug that was never removed may be the cause. When we replace a disposal, we reconnect the dishwasher drain and test it before leaving. A licensed plumber makes that connection to the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC) as adopted in Colorado, so a hose routed badly can trouble an inspection.

The wall switch and its wiring are the other half of the system. A failed switch looks like a dead disposal. Electrical work beyond a simple switch swap may need a licensed electrician, and whether a permit is needed differs by job, so ask the Town's building division. A dripping kitchen tap near the same sink is a separate job, covered under faucet repair.

Keep the disposal out of the clog

A disposal moves scraps to the drain, but it does not clear the pipe beyond it. Grease, starchy scraps like rice and potato peels, and coffee grounds settle in the trap and the line. If the sink backs up even though the unit runs, the clog is past the disposal, and a visit for drain cleaning is the better call. For repeated kitchen backups, hydro jetting is worth asking about.
