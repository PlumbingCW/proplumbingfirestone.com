How to Install a Toilet in Firestone, CO (And When Not To)

By the Firestone Pro Plumbing team. Published October 16, 2026. Updated October 16, 2026.

Talk to a plumber
(303) 552-3896
Call Now

Yes, swapping a toilet is one of the easier plumbing jobs, if the floor flange is in good shape and the new bowl is the right fit. Most of the work is lifting a heavy fixture and getting a clean seal.

The trouble starts when the flange is cracked, the floor is soft, or the shutoff valve will not close. If you see any of that, stop and call (303) 552-3896 before you set the bowl.

Easy job or hidden trouble: how to tell before you start

Look at the situation first. The same job can be an afternoon project or a repair that needs a trade.

Good DIY candidates

  • Shutoff valve turns fully and stops the water.
  • Flange sits flat on the finished floor and is not cracked.
  • Floor around the toilet is firm and dry.
  • New toilet matches the rough-in distance.
  • You can lift the bowl without straining.

Call a plumber first

  • Valve is stuck, leaking, or will not turn.
  • Flange is broken, rusted, or below the floor surface.
  • Floor feels soft or shows water stains.
  • Toilet rocks and the floor is uneven.
  • Drain smells or gurgles after flushing.

Check the rough-in before you buy anything

The rough-in is the distance from the finished back wall to the center of the drain. Measure it before you shop, because a toilet built for a different measurement will not sit right. Many bowls fit the common measurement, but not all do.

Also check the shape. Round bowls and elongated bowls need different space in front. Measure from the wall to the tip of where the bowl would end and compare to the box dimensions.

Install a toilet in six plain steps

Work with a helper if the bowl is heavy. A cracked bowl or a pinched back is a bad way to end the day.

  1. Shut off the water.Close the supply valve, flush, and hold the handle down to empty the tank. Sponge out what remains.
  2. Disconnect and lift.Undo the supply line and the two nuts at the base. Rock the bowl gently and lift it straight up.
  3. Inspect the flange.Scrape off old wax. Look for cracks, rust, and loose bolts. Stuff a rag in the drain to block sewer gas.
  4. Set the new ring and bowl.Place a fresh wax ring, lower the bowl straight down onto the bolts, and press with steady weight.
  5. Tighten in turns.Snug each nut a little at a time. Over-tightening cracks porcelain.
  6. Reconnect and test.Attach the supply, open the valve slowly, flush several times, and look for drips at the base and the connection.

The wax ring is small but it does the sealing

A wax ring seals the joint between the toilet and the drain. It stops sewer gas and water from reaching the floor. Never reuse an old one. Once a ring is squashed, it will not seal again.

If a toilet rocks, the ring can crack. If you smell sewage, or see water at the base after a flush, the seal has likely failed. Do not just add more wax. Lift the bowl and find out why it moved.

Flange problems and what each one usually means

The flange is the ring on the floor that the bolts anchor to. It is where most surprises are hiding.

A bowl set on a bad flange will leak again, so fix the flange first.

Choosing a toilet that fits the room

Height matters for comfort, and bowl shape matters for space. A taller bowl suits knees and hips, while a compact one fits a small bathroom. Bring the measurements with you to the store. Clearances around the bowl matter too, and the base code licensed plumbers follow is the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC) as adopted in Colorado, so measure the room before you buy.

Check the tank bolts and the fill valve. Most come in the box, but some need a different supply line size. If your shutoff valve is the old compression type, a new valve may be easier than fighting an adapter.

When a swap turns into a repair

Sometimes you pull the bowl and find damage you could not see before. A soft subfloor, a broken flange, or a stuck valve changes the job. Flooring under old toilets can rot slowly from small leaks, and you do not know until the toilet is up.

Our toilet repair page covers leaks, running tanks, and cracked bowls in more detail. If the problem spreads past the toilet, such as a new bathroom layout or moved drains, that is closer to plumbing installation work with different rules.

Tools and parts to set out before you lift the old bowl

Having everything within reach avoids a toilet-less bathroom overnight.

  • New wax ring and bolts.Buy a fresh set even if the new toilet includes one.
  • New supply line.Braided lines are easier to bend and seal.
  • Putty knife and rags.For scraping old wax and blocking the drain opening.
  • Adjustable wrench.Plus a small level to check the bowl is not leaning.
  • Bucket and sponge.For leftover water in the tank and bowl.

What this means in Firestone, CO

The original townsite plat from 1907 covers homes far older, where the drain, flange, and floor may all be older than the toilet you are replacing. If your house sits in that part of town, plan for the chance of surprises under the old bowl.

Plumbing permit rules vary, so ask the Firestone building division whether a straight fixture swap needs one for your address. Replacing the toilet in the same spot is different from moving it. If you have a rural property near Mead, ask whether it is on septic, since a failing drain can mimic a bad seal. A toilet swap in Erie raises the same permit question, and that town's building department is the one to ask.
