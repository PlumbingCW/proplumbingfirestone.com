PEX Pipe in Residential Plumbing: What Firestone, CO Homeowners Should Know

By the Firestone Pro Plumbing team. Published October 16, 2026. Updated October 16, 2026.

Talk to a plumber
(303) 552-3896
Call Now

PEX is flexible plastic pipe used for hot and cold water supply inside homes. It resists corrosion, bends around corners, and goes in with fewer joints than rigid pipe. It is not perfect. Sunlight damages it, it needs the right fittings, and some jobs still call for metal.

For most supply-line work in a house, PEX is a sound choice when installed with matching parts. Whether your job is allowed as planned is a code question for the Firestone building division.

What the letters stand for and why the pipe bends

PEX stands for cross-linked polyethylene. The cross-linking is a chemical or physical process that ties the plastic chains together, which gives the pipe its flexibility and its heat tolerance.

You will see it in red for hot, blue for cold, and white or gray for either. The color is only a label. It does not change how the pipe performs.

The pipe comes in coils, so a long run can go from a manifold to a fixture with no joints in the wall at all. Fewer joints means fewer places for a leak to start.

The honest list of strengths and weaknesses

No pipe is right for every job. Here is the plain version.

Where PEX is strong

  • Does not rust or pit the way old steel pipe does.
  • Bends around framing, so fewer elbows are needed.
  • Quick to install, which often lowers labor.
  • Handles a hard freeze better than rigid pipe.
  • Quieter than metal when water hammers.

Where PEX falls short

  • Breaks down in sunlight, so it cannot sit outside.
  • Needs its own tools and fittings, and mixing brands can fail.
  • Not for direct connection to a water heater without a code-approved section.
  • Can be chewed or punctured, and fittings can still leak.
  • Plastic is harder to recycle than copper.

Fitting types and what to watch for with each

The fitting matters as much as the pipe. Each system has its own tool.

Never mix parts from different systems unless the maker approves it. A fitting that looks like it fits can still leak months later.

What happens when PEX freezes

PEX can stretch as ice forms inside it, and it often survives a freeze that would split copper. Often is not always. Fittings and rigid sections nearby can still crack, and a pipe stretched more than once may weaken.

Treat PEX as more forgiving, not freeze-proof. Keep it away from exterior walls and unheated spaces where you can, and insulate what you cannot move.

Sunlight and PEX do not mix

Ultraviolet light weakens the plastic. A run left in the open during construction, or placed along an outside wall, can become brittle. Makers set limits on how long the pipe may be exposed before it is covered.

If you see PEX in a garage window, an open crawlspace, or an exterior wall without cover, ask a plumber to look at it. Where a pipe must be outside, it needs a protective sleeve or a different material.

Chlorine matters too. PEX products carry a chlorine resistance rating. Ask your plumber to confirm the product you use is rated for the water in your area.

Manifold systems and branch lines

There are two common layouts. A branch system runs one main line with tees to each fixture, much like copper. A manifold system, sometimes called home run, sends a separate line from a central box to every fixture.

Manifolds use more pipe but give each fixture its own supply. One shut-off on the manifold can isolate a single bathroom. That makes a later repair far less disruptive, because you are not shutting the whole house down to fix one sink.

If your house has a manifold, learn where it is and what each valve controls. It is worth a label with a marker.

Before anyone swaps a run for PEX

Use this order for any planned change.

  2. Choose one system.Pick a single maker and one fitting style and keep to it for the whole job.
  3. Plan the route.Keep the pipe out of sunlight, away from sharp edges, and clear of heat sources.

  5. Pressure test.Test every joint before drywall closes the wall.

When a house needs more than a repair

PEX is common for a full repipe of an older house that still has galvanized steel. It is also common in new work, where it is run from a manifold to each fixture. Our plumbing installation page covers new lines and fixtures.

Repair is different. A single pinhole in copper can often be fixed with one fitting, and not every old pipe needs replacing. If you are unsure, have a plumber trace the problem before tearing into walls.

What this means in Firestone, CO

Firestone has grown from 1,908 residents in 2000 to 16,381 in 2020, so most homes in town were built after 2000. That means many already have modern supply piping, and a full repipe is more likely in original townsite homes or where one run has failed.

For the code question, the base standard is the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC) as adopted in Colorado. Licensed plumbers work to the IPC, and the pipe markings should name the potable water standard the product meets, so read them before you buy. The Firestone building division can say whether your job needs a permit. Call the Town or email Building@FirestoneCO.gov.

Rural properties near Mead in unincorporated Weld County follow the Weld County Building Department, not the Town. In Erie the same product question applies, and the town's own building department answers the code side.
