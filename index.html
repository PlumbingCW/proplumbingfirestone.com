Do this first

Do these things while you wait for the plumber

Most of the damage in a plumbing emergency happens in the first stretch of time after the leak starts. A few steps from you can limit it before anyone arrives.

Shut off the main water valve.

It is usually where the water line enters the house, often in a utility area, garage or basement. Turn it clockwise until it stops. If you cannot find it, tell the dispatcher.

Stop using drains and toilets.

A sewer backup pushes wastewater up through the lowest fixture. Every flush or sink load adds to it, so wait until a plumber has cleared the line.

Keep away from wet electrical areas.

Do not step into standing water near outlets, a panel or plugged-in appliances. If you can reach the breaker safely from a dry spot, switch off that area.

Smell gas? Leave first.

A gas smell near a water heater or furnace means everyone goes outside. Call the gas utility or 911 from outside, then call us about the plumbing.

Move what you can, take photos.

Lift rugs, boxes and electronics off wet floors. Take a few photos of the damage, because your insurer may ask for them later.

Plumbing services we dispatch in Firestone

The most common calls are a stubborn clog that needs drain cleaning or a pipe that has split and needs burst pipe repair. When a snake will not clear a line full of grease or roots, a plumber may recommend hydro jetting, which scours the pipe wall with high-pressure water.

Water heaters generate their own urgent calls. A tank that is leaking at the base often needs water heater replacement, while a heater that simply fails to heat may be a candidate for water heater repair. Planned upgrades such as a tankless water heater are scheduled work instead.

Hidden problems need a different approach. A bill that climbs for no reason points toward leak detection, and a main line that keeps backing up calls for sewer line repair once a camera shows what is wrong. The full list is on our services page.

Licensed plumbers, and what that means here

Colorado licenses plumbers at the state level through DORA, the Department of Regulatory Agencies. Anyone can check a plumber's license through DORA's online lookup, and we encourage you to do that for any contractor you hire.

The Town of Firestone also expects permit applications for licensed work to list the plumber and the state license number, and inspectors can fail an inspection if they cannot verify it. Licensed plumbers work to the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC) as adopted in Colorado, which the Town enforces through permits and inspections. Permit rules vary by job, so ask the Firestone building division whether yours needs one.

What Firestone homes mean for your plumbing

Firestone grew from about 10,000 residents in 2010 to more than 16,000 in 2020, so most homes in town were built after 2000. Newer houses tend to have plastic supply lines and modern fittings, which means many calls involve a failed connection or a water heater, not corroded pipe. The original townsite homes near Grant Avenue and Fourth Street are far older, so ask about pipe age if you own one.
