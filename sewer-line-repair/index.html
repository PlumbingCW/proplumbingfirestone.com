A sewer line that has cracked, collapsed, or shifted lets wastewater back up into the house or leak into the soil, and our 24/7 emergency line takes those calls. Sewer line repair in Firestone, CO means fixing or replacing the failed section of pipe that carries waste from your home to the municipal main. Until we arrive, use no water, stay out of the wet area, and keep hoses away from every drain.

Repair is a different job from clearing. If a cable or jet opens the line and it stops up again soon, the pipe itself is usually the problem. A camera tells us where the damage is and why it happened.

A sewer backup that keeps returning

One backup can be a wad of paper. A second backup within a few weeks, in the same fixtures, almost never is. At that point we stop treating it as a clog and start looking for a broken, sagging, or root-filled section of pipe.

The pattern helps us. A blockage that returns at the same spot points to a single failed joint. Slow drains across the whole house point to a pipe that has lost its shape along a longer run. Tell us what the house has been doing, because that history narrows the search before the camera goes in.

If the line is blocked right now, we may need to open it first so the camera can see. That is a sewer line cleaning step, and it is usually part of the same visit.

Dig it up or line it

The camera video decides this, not a preference.

Open trench makes sense when

  • The pipe is collapsed, crushed, or badly offset.
  • A section sags and holds water.
  • Roots have destroyed the pipe wall.
  • A short dig reaches the problem easily.

Lining makes sense when

  • The pipe is round and mostly intact.
  • Damage is spread along a long run.
  • Digging would tear up a driveway, patio, or mature trees.
  • Access from a cleanout is good.

What actually breaks a sewer line

Roots.

They slip into a joint and keep growing, which cracks the pipe wall and catches debris. A jetting pass clears them only for a while.

Offset joints.

When two pipe sections shift apart, a lip forms that catches paper and slows flow.

Corroded cast iron.

Old cast iron rusts from the inside out and flakes into the stream.

Bellies.

A low spot holds water and solids, so the line fills and then stops.

Crushed pipe.

Heavy loads over a shallow section or soil settling can flatten the pipe.

Soil movement.

Ground that swells and shrinks can pull on a rigid pipe, and joints are where it gives.

Cast iron and clay in older Firestone homes

The original townsite was platted in 1907, from Grant Avenue to McClure Avenue and from First Street to Fourth Street. Houses there are far older than most of town, so the sewer lateral may be cast iron or clay with many joints. More joints means more places for roots to get in, and we plan on a camera look before quoting a fix.

Most Firestone homes were built after 2000, and their sewer pipe is usually newer material. Failures there more often come from a bad joint, a sag from poor backfill, or soil that settled. Homes in ZIP codes 80504 and 80520 mix both eras, so the age of the house is the first thing we ask.

Permit rules vary, so ask the Firestone building division before any dig. The Town checks that permitted plumbing work lists a licensed plumber, and you can verify any plumber on the state DORA license lookup. Repairs follow the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC) as adopted in Colorado, the base code the Town enforces through permits and inspections.

Soil, sprinklers, and downspouts around the sewer

The Colorado Geological Survey calls expansive clay the state's most significant geologic hazard. Soil can swell by 10 percent or more when wet, and the usual cause is surface water and irrigation soaking in beside a foundation. That is a statewide statement, not a ruling on your lot.

What it means for a sewer line is practical. Pipe that sits in soil that swells and shrinks sees stress at the joints, so a sprinkler head that soaks one side of the house every morning is worth fixing.

We do not diagnose soil movement, since a soil engineer does. We do fix the pipe, and we talk about drainage so the repair does not fail for the same reason, and our basement waterproofing page covers the plumbing side of it.

If your property is on septic

Not every property in the area is on a sewer main. Some rural parcels around Mead and in unincorporated Weld County may use a septic tank and drain field, and the repair path is different. The Weld County Building Department covers codes for unincorporated parcels.

Tell us at the start of the call if your property is on septic. A backed-up septic system and a failed sewer lateral can look alike, and the right plumber depends on the answer.
