Drain cleaning means clearing the blockage from a fixture drain or branch line so water moves freely again.

Most calls fall into two groups. One fixture that is slow or stopped usually means a local clog. Several fixtures acting up together point to the main line, which is a different job covered on our main sewer clearing page.

Tell us on the phone which drain is affected and what else in the house is acting strange. Those two details let the plumber arrive with the right cable and the right head.

Do this first

Drain backing up right now? Do these first

None of these steps fixes the clog. They keep a bad hour from becoming a bad week.

Stop adding water.

Turn off the washer, the dishwasher, and any running tap. Every gallon sent into a blocked line has to go somewhere, and that somewhere is usually your floor.

Contain the overflow.

Towels around the base of a tub or toilet buy time. Lift small rugs and move anything valuable away from the drain.

Skip the bottled opener.

Caustic liquid sits in a stopped pipe and can splash when the line opens. If you already poured some in, tell us so the plumber wears the right gear.

Mind the electrical.

Stay away from outlets, cords, and appliances that are standing in water. If water is spreading toward a panel, stay clear and tell us when you call.

Gas smell means leave.

A rotten-egg odor is not a drain smell. Get everyone outside and phone the gas utility or 911 ahead of us.

A kitchen sink that will not drain

The kitchen line is the clog we hear about most, and grease is the usual cause. Cooking fat goes down warm and liquid, then cools into a coating on the pipe wall. Over months that coating catches coffee grounds, rice, and scraps until the opening is nearly shut.

Standing water on one side of a double sink often means the clog sits right under the strainer or in the trap. Water that rises in the second basin when the dishwasher runs points farther down the branch. A disposal that hums but will not spin is a separate problem, and our disposal repair page covers it.

We pull the trap first, then run a cable toward the stack. Central Weld County Water District reports average hardness of 33 mg/L, which is soft, so in a home it serves a kitchen blockage is almost always grease and food rather than mineral scale. Dirt, leaves, and fine roots pack these. They show up after a heavy rain when water pools instead of leaving.

Snaking, cabling, and when we stop using them

A hand snake works for a trap or a short run. A powered cable reaches into longer branch lines and cuts through hair, paper, and softer grease. We pick the head for what is blocking the pipe, not out of habit.

Some blockages beat a cable. A tight mat of roots or a hard grease collar leaves a hole the size of the cable, and the line closes again within weeks. When we see that pattern, we talk with you about hydro jetting, which scours the full pipe wall with pressurized water.

Our aim is to open the line and say plainly what we found, including when the cable is only a short-term fix.

Why bottled openers backfire

Caustic openers work on a thin film at the top of a clog. In a fully stopped pipe they pool, heat the pipe, and wait for the next person to touch it. A plastic trap can soften, A trap that has softened gets replaced with a new one that meets the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC) as adopted in Colorado, the base code licensed plumbers work to. and the plumber ends up cabling through chemical water.

A plain plunger and a pot of hot tap water on a greasy trap is gentler. Stop when water rises past the overflow, and call us.

When the drain pipe itself leaks

A drain line that weeps at a joint under a sink or in a crawl space is a different problem from a clog. Dampness around the pipe, a musty smell, or a stain on the ceiling below a bathroom all point to it. Cabling will not fix a loose or cracked joint, so we repair the section or run a camera to see how far the damage goes.
