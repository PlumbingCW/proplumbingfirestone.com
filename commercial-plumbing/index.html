Commercial plumbing in Firestone, CO means repair, clearing and installation work on the water, drain and waste systems of businesses, rental buildings and shared properties. Our 24/7 emergency line takes calls when a pipe lets go in a closed building or a floor drain backs up in the middle of service. While you wait, close the nearest shutoff, keep customers and staff off wet floors, and stop running any sink that drains toward the problem.

Commercial jobs differ from house calls in ways that matter on the phone. Lines run longer, one shutoff may serve several tenants, and some fixtures sit behind locked doors or ceiling grids. Tell the dispatcher the building type, which tenant is affected, and whether you can reach the main valve.

We work for businesses in ZIP codes 80504 and 80520 and the towns around them. Below we describe the scope of each kind of job. We do not promise results for a particular building until a licensed plumber has looked at it.

Water on the floor of your business right now

Do these in order. Each one limits damage before a plumber is on site.

Shut off the water.

Find the valve for your suite or the building main. If a shared valve serves other tenants, tell the property manager before you close it.

Keep people out of the wet area.

Water near outlets, panels or ceiling lights can energize a floor. Cut power to that area only if the breaker is reachable without stepping in water.

Stop using backing-up drains.

If a restroom or floor drain is overflowing, stop flushing and running sinks. Extra water only adds to the mess.

Leave if you smell gas.

A gas smell means everyone leaves the building. Call the gas utility or 911 first, then call us.

Photograph the damage.

Pictures of the water, the shutoff and any stained ceiling help the plumber plan and help with any later insurance claim.

What commercial plumbing covers and where it stops

The work includes restroom fixtures, supply lines, drain and waste lines, water heaters sized for a business, floor drains, grease interceptors and backflow devices. It also covers the hookups behind a tenant build-out, such as a prep sink, a mop sink or a break room line.

Some water problems are not plumbing. We do not repair roof drainage, fire sprinkler systems or structural damage left behind by a leak. A plumber can tell you where the plumbing ends and who to call for the rest.

Colorado licenses plumbers at the state level through DORA, and anyone can check a license on its online lookup. On permitted work the Town of Firestone asks that the plumber's license be listed and verified, and inspectors may fail an inspection they cannot verify. Plumbers work to the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC) as adopted in Colorado, which the building division checks at inspection. Ask for the license details before work begins so your inspection does not stall.

Commercial leak detection: scope, access and after-hours visits

A search for a hidden leak starts with questions. Where is the damp spot, when did it begin, and does the meter move when the building is closed? From there a plumber narrows it down with listening equipment, thermal imaging, pressure tests and, for drains, a camera.

Access shapes the job more than the tools do. Ceilings, mechanical rooms and tenant spaces may need keys or a manager's permission. Evenings and weekends are often the easiest time to test a line, because the building is quiet and water use can be paused.

We describe what each method can find and we do not promise to locate every leak before the plumber has seen the layout. The leak detection page explains the methods in more detail.

Grease traps, interceptors and kitchen drains

Food service sends grease down the drain every shift. A trap or interceptor catches it, but only while it is emptied and cleaned on a schedule.

Slow kitchen drains.

Often the first sign that the trap is full or the line behind it is coating with fat.

Sour smell near the lid.

A sharp odor around the trap or nearby floor drain usually means it is overdue for service.

Pumping and cleaning.

Emptying the unit and scrubbing the baffles. Disposal rules can differ, so ask the Firestone building division and your sewer provider what applies to your space.

Backups that keep returning.

A camera look or hydro jetting can clear the line past the trap when a snake only buys a few days.

Floor drain overflow in a shop, garage or back-of-house

A floor drain is a short line with a trap, and it fills from the main when the main is blocked. When water rises in the grate, the trouble is usually downstream of it. Clearing only the grate does little if the main line is the real issue.

A rarely used drain can also dry out and lose its water seal, which lets sewer smell into the room. Pouring in a bucket of water restores the seal. If water will not go down at all, stop and call.

New construction rough-in and backflow devices

Rough-in is the pipe work set before walls and floors close: drain and vent lines, supply piping and capped stub-outs for each future fixture. On a new build or tenant finish-out it is scheduled around framing and inspection, so bring a plumber in before the drywall crew. Permit rules vary, so ask the Firestone building division what your project needs.

Many commercial properties also carry a backflow device that keeps dirty water from flowing back into the supply. These devices often need periodic testing, and the notice usually comes from your water provider. Firestone buys treated water from supplier districts, so ask which one serves your address and who sends your notice. The plumbing installation page covers rough-in and hookups.
