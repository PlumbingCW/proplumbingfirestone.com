Faucet repair in Firestone, CO covers dripping spouts, worn cartridges, leaking handles, shower valves and the small shutoff valves under sinks. It becomes an emergency when a valve will not close and water keeps running. Our 24/7 line takes those calls, and the first move while you wait is the main shutoff for the house.

Every fixture has two ways to stop the water: its own valve and the main. Under a sink you will find two small handles. If they spin without slowing the flow, or feel gritty and stiff, leave them alone and close the main instead.

Houses in ZIP codes 80504 and 80520 mostly date from after 2000, so many faucet problems here come from worn cartridges and supply valves, not rusted pipe. Cartridges are model-specific, so read the brand off the fixture or take a photo before you call.

Where the drip is coming from tells you the repair

Water at the spout tip usually means a worn cartridge, washer or valve seat inside the faucet body. Water at the base of the handle points to an O-ring or a loose packing nut. Water under the sink may be a cracked supply line or a weeping valve, and a wet cabinet floor deserves a look soon.

Water inside a wall behind a shower or tub points to the valve body or the pipe feeding it. That is the one drip we ask you to treat as urgent, because it can stain and rot framing before you see it.

Valve stuck open: what to do first

Close the main shutoff.

Turn the main valve clockwise until it stops. If you do not know where it is, check near the water heater or where the line enters the house.

Open the lowest faucet.

This drains the pipes and takes pressure off the broken fixture.

Do not force a stuck handle.

A corroded stem can snap, and a small leak becomes a spray. If water is jetting from a wall, see burst pipe repair.

Contain and protect.

Use towels and a bucket, and keep cords and power strips away from standing water.

Broken supply valves under the sink

The small valve under a sink is rarely moved, so it often seizes. When someone finally turns it, the stem can leak or crack. A valve that cannot be closed means the whole house loses water while the faucet or supply line is replaced. Licensed plumbers work to the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC) as adopted in Colorado, which expects fixtures to have working shutoffs, so a seized one is worth replacing.

A plumber can swap the valve, the supply line and the fixture in one visit when parts are on hand. Replacing an old valve while the pipes are dry is cheaper than doing it again after it fails. Tell us whether the leak is at the handle, the pipe connection or the wall.

Isolating a single fixture before we start

Do this only if the small valves turn easily.

  1. Find the two handles.Hot is usually on the left and cold on the right.
  2. Turn each clockwise.Quarter turns are enough on newer valves. Stop if you feel resistance.
  3. Open the faucet.The flow should drop to nothing within a few seconds.
  4. Call if water keeps coming.A valve that fails this test needs replacement, and the main shutoff stays closed meanwhile.

Showers, tubs and outdoor spigots

Dripping shower valve.

Usually a worn cartridge. The wall is opened only if the valve body must be replaced.

Showerhead leak.

Often a loose thread or a worn washer, which is a quick job.

Tub spout and diverter.

If water comes out both spout and showerhead, the diverter has failed.

Slow tub or shower drain.

Hair is the usual cause. A deeper clog may need drain cleaning.

Outdoor hose spigot.

A drip behind the handle is a packing issue. Water inside the wall in cold months is a frozen-pipe sign.

Utility sink or jetted tub.

These share the same cartridge and valve problems, with extra access to plan.

Repair the faucet or replace it

If a fixture is old and parts are no longer made, a new faucet can cost less than hunting for a cartridge. If it is newer, replacing the cartridge restores it. The plumber will tell you which makes sense once the handle is off.

A new faucet is also a chance to think about drinking water. The Town's water quality report advises running taps for several minutes after water has sat, and mentions an ANSI-certified filter for lead. See water filtration options if that matters to you.
