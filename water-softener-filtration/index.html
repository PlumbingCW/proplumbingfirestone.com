Per its 2025 report, Central Weld County Water District averaged 33 mg/L of hardness, a level it calls soft, since anything under 60 mg/L counts as soft. That matters for anyone shopping for a water softener in Firestone, CO. A softener removes hardness minerals, so if your water has few, a softener may add cost and maintenance without much benefit.

Filtration answers a different question. A filter targets specific things such as chlorine taste, sediment or lead, and each filter type has its own limits. The sensible order is to learn what is in your water, then choose the equipment, and a licensed plumber can install either one.

Residents in ZIP codes 80504 and 80520 may be on different supply sources, so check which one serves your address before spending anything.

Do you need a softener at all?

Hardness is the dissolved calcium and magnesium that leaves scale on heaters, faucets and glass. At 33 mg/L, CWCWD water sits well under the district's own line for soft water, so heavy scale buildup is less likely for homes it serves. A softener there would mostly address a problem that may not exist. A tankless water heater also scales more slowly in soft water, though it still needs flushing.

Test before you buy. A simple test strip or lab sample tells you your hardness, and the report from your supplier says what the water is when it leaves the plant.

The district's hardness figure is public. We have not seen a hardness number for the other supplier, so we will not guess one.

Look at your water bill for the supplier name, or ask the Town. Then read that supplier's water quality report. A home on groundwater may have different hardness from a home on district water, and that decides whether softening makes sense. The Central Weld County Water District water quality report is the guideline to check before a salesperson quotes you a system.

Softening versus filtering

These solve different problems and are often confused.

A softener does this

  • Swaps hardness minerals for sodium or potassium.
  • Reduces scale in pipes, heaters and fixtures.
  • Needs salt refills and periodic valve service.
  • Does not remove lead, chlorine or sediment.

A filter does this

  • Removes specific contaminants, depending on the media.
  • Improves taste and odor when carbon is used.
  • Needs cartridges replaced on a schedule.
  • Does not remove hardness unless paired with a softener.

The lead notice from the Town's water report

The Town's 2024 report, covering 2023 data, shows a lead 90th percentile of 2.1 ppb, against an action level of 15 ppb. We cannot say whether your house has a lead service line, and the report does not say either. The Town advises running a tap for several minutes if water has sat, and considering an ANSI-certified filter for lead.

Take that as practical advice, not alarm. Flush the cold tap before drinking after a long absence, and if you add a filter, check that the package names an ANSI certification for lead. A softener will not remove lead, so one filter is not a substitute for the other.

Repairing an existing softener or conditioner

Many homes already have a unit. These faults come up most often.

Hard water again.

The salt may be bridged or the resin tired.

Control valve problems.

A valve that will not cycle or keeps running can fail to regenerate.

Brine tank trouble.

Salt crust, a clogged line or a float stuck in the tank.

Leaks at fittings.

Bypass valves and drain lines can seep at connections.

Low pressure after install.

A clogged resin bed or inlet screen can restrict flow.
