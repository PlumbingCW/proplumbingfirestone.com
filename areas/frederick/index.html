If water is running where it should not be in Frederick

These steps matter more in Frederick than you might expect, because the valve you reach first is not always yours to repair.

Close the valve where the line enters the house.

Look near the water heater or in a utility area and turn the handle clockwise until it stops. If it spins loose or leaks at the stem, leave it alone and tell us. The next valve is at the meter, and that one belongs to your water provider.

Note who sends your water bill.

Pick up a recent bill and read the name at the top. Town of Frederick, a district, or something else, that name tells us who to point you toward if the leak is on the street side of the meter.

Cut power near anything wet.

If water has reached outlets, a power strip or the panel, leave the room alone and shut off power only if the switch is dry and you can reach it without stepping in water.

Photograph before you mop.

A few pictures of the wet wall, ceiling or floor help your insurer and help us read the damage. Then move rugs and electronics out of the path.

Smell gas? Leave first.

A gas odor near a water heater means everyone outside, then the gas utility or 911, then us.

The 1907 streets and the newer blocks around them

The original Frederick townsite was laid out in 1907, and the town has grown fast since. Between 2010 and 2020 the population climbed from 8,679 to 14,513. That means an older core surrounded by far more recent homes, and the two fail in different ways.

On the older streets we ask about the shutoff valve and the supply pipe material whenever we are in the house for something else. A valve that has sat untouched for decades may not close all the way. Learning that in the middle of a burst is the worst timing, so a planned check beats a flooded floor.

In a newer house the usual trouble is a single failed part, like a supply line, a fixture valve or a water heater connection. When a wet spot has no obvious source, acoustic and thermal leak detection can find the failing run without opening every wall.
